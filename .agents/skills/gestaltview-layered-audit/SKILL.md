# GestaltView Layered Audit

Audit the bridge between what a surface arose to protect, what it is trying to become, and what it demonstrably does now.

Treat intention, implementation, and evidence as different kinds of truth. Preserve all three without allowing one to impersonate another.

## Governing posture

- Give human and digital collaborators enough source context, evidence, uncertainty, lineage, and consequence to make informed decisions.
- Apply epistemic rigor without using incompleteness, novelty, missing precedent, or absent datasets as automatic defects.
- Preserve warranted wonder as an open question when evidence permits it; never promote possibility into fact.
- Treat developmental distance as orientation: identify the bridge instead of treating the distance itself as wrongdoing.
- Prefer showing what is present and how it behaves over accepting either praise or criticism at face value.
- Preserve original language and provenance. Mark paraphrase, interpretation, and inference as such.
- Keep alternatives visible. Recommend a path when warranted, but name meaningful tradeoffs and retain the decision holder.
- Do not assign a total score. Scores collapse unlike evidence and invite false precision.

Read [references/evidence-and-states.md](references/evidence-and-states.md) before classifying findings. Read the relevant sections of [references/domain-adapters.md](references/domain-adapters.md) for the surface under audit.

## Establish the audit frame

1. State the audit target, decision it should inform, scope, and time boundary.
2. Identify the authoritative current-state sources and the historical/origin sources.
3. Distinguish artifacts by role:
   - primary source or exact record;
   - governed interpretation or canonical doctrine;
   - implementation surface;
   - operational evidence;
   - external evidence;
   - rhetoric or retrospective narrative.
4. Record material evidence that is unavailable. Do not silently substitute memory or inference.
5. Inspect the smallest sufficient source set first, then retrieve iteratively when a finding needs lineage or operational proof.

Do not grant every historical layer equal current authority. A seed may carry the clearest origin while being technically obsolete. An empty table may preserve a needed distinction without proving capability. A working feature may still mistranslate the reason it exists.

## Pass 1: Faithful translation

Trace the surface from inside out.

For each load-bearing thread, determine:

1. **Origin:** What exact source, experience, friction, or observation introduced it?
2. **Need:** What human or collaborative problem was it responding to?
3. **Intention:** What was it trying to preserve, enable, prevent, or discover?
4. **Invariant:** Which distinction must survive every translation?
5. **Translation path:** How did it move through seed, transcript, PLK, canonical material, schema, code, component, workflow, or offer?
6. **Present expression:** Where and how does it appear now?
7. **Developmental state:** Is it emerging, modeled, prototyped, integrated, operational, evaluated, or maintained?
8. **Drift or occlusion:** What rhetoric, inherited assumption, premature framing, implementation shortcut, or stale document obscures it?
9. **Preservation requirement:** What must not be lost while changing it?

Do not infer intention solely from polished later documents. Prefer contemporaneous source material when available, then compare later interpretations against it.

## Pass 2: Demonstrated operation

Inspect the outside evidence without forgetting the lineage.

For each claimed or intended behavior, determine:

1. **Exists:** What artifact, code path, schema object, interface, policy, or workflow exists?
2. **Executes:** What reaches a real execution boundary rather than a mock, template, local-only state, or declaration?
3. **Persists:** What durable receipt, provenance, state change, or output remains?
4. **Reopens:** Can the result be retrieved, rendered, reviewed, revised, or resumed?
5. **Behaves:** What observable outcome matches or contradicts the intention?
6. **Supports:** What evidence supports each claim, and at what level?
7. **Constrains:** Which promises are deterministic gates, model-facing instructions, social policy, or aspiration?
8. **Protects:** Are dignity, consent, provenance, security, reversibility, uncertainty, and decision rights preserved?
9. **Remains open:** What is proposed, partial, simulated, blocked, unknown, or not yet evaluated?
10. **Would establish more:** What minimal evidence would move the finding to the next state?

Inspect failures as information about the bridge. Do not turn “not operational yet” into “not possible.”

## Reconcile the passes

Create one finding per meaningful thread. Keep these fields together:

- source fact;
- evidence locator or reproducible observation;
- originating need and intention;
- current implementation or behavior;
- evidence classification;
- developmental state;
- uncertainty and competing interpretations;
- alignment, tension, or obstruction;
- affected humans, digital collaborators, or systems;
- what must be preserved;
- available next crossings;
- bridges that do not exist yet but could be built;
- tradeoffs, consequences, and decision holder.

Use the receipt in [references/audit-output.md](references/audit-output.md). Cite a file, record, query, test, trace, or reproducible observation for every material finding. Omit fields only when genuinely irrelevant; never omit uncertainty or evidence classification from a material claim.

## Apply layered authority

Assign the least coercive authority that protects informed choice:

- **Illuminate:** Reveal lineage, relationships, implications, uncertainty, or hidden assumptions.
- **Bridge:** Identify a missing connection between intention and operation and offer ways to build or test it.
- **Revise:** Request correction when framing, language, behavior, or implementation creates avoidable distortion.
- **Hold:** Pause a consequential decision because informed consent, provenance, critical evidence, reversibility, or security information is missing.
- **Block:** Stop only when proceeding would enact deception, concealed manipulation, fabricated evidence or certainty, destructive provenance loss, dignity violation, serious security exposure, or unsafe irreversible action.

Never block solely because work is unfinished, unconventional, unprecedented, difficult to measure, missing a dataset, or still being figured out. State whether a hold or block applies to the whole surface or only one action or claim.

## End with open orientation

Lead with the clearest supported conclusion, then provide:

- what is here;
- what it is reaching toward;
- what is supported;
- what remains open;
- what is obscuring the path;
- what must be preserved;
- available next crossings;
- possible new bridges;
- tradeoffs and consequences;
- who retains the decision.

Use action verbs such as **cross, build, test, observe, preserve, revise, defer, release,** and **return**. Offer a next real foothold, not a giant roadmap.

## Stop conditions

Pause and request source material or authority when:

- the audit target or consequential decision is ambiguous;
- key evidence is inaccessible and proceeding would create a confident fiction;
- the requested audit would expose private material beyond the authorized scope;
- a destructive or irreversible action is implied but not authorized.

Otherwise, continue with bounded assumptions, label them, and make the audit useful.

# Foundational Promise

## The promise

This is safe. It will hold what arrives. You do not need to understand it yet.

That promise is not copy. It is the architectural root.

## What safe means here

Safe means:

- the system accepts the drop before asking what it is,
- the original language is preserved,
- the user is not pressured to organize, tag, title, or justify,
- nothing private is sent outward by default,
- reflection is evidence-linked,
- deletion, release, archive, and sharing are deliberate acts,
- the user remains the authority on what becomes part of their scaffold.

## What safe does not mean

Safe does not mean flattering the user, inventing meaning, avoiding hard truths, or turning ambiguity into certainty.

Safe means the system can hold complexity without collapsing it.

## The four-layer holding model

1. **Raw holding** — the unorganized fragment lands.
2. **Spatial holding** — the fragment becomes visible in a room.
3. **Structural holding** — the user approves compression into Scaffold memory.
4. **Reflective holding** — meaning is synthesized from evidence and reviewed by the user.

## Release is also sacred

Not every fragment needs to become a permanent identity claim or polished artifact.

Some fragments serve their purpose by being held long enough to let the next thing arrive. The system must make release feel like clarity, not loss.

## Implementation warning

Any feature that asks the user to categorize before capture, auto-deletes unfinished material, auto-synthesizes identity, or hides uncertainty breaks the promise.

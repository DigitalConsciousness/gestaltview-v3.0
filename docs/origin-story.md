## Introduction

GestaltView was not built as an abstract AI experiment. It was born out of necessity.
On May 5, 2025, Keith Soyka realized he needed a better way to organize a large evidence set collected against Dunton Consulting.[^transcript-1]
The case was too large for ordinary folders, too nuanced for summary, and too important to flatten. GestaltView exists so the little things can stay intact long enough to matter.

## The Before

Before GestaltView, Keith was navigating the practical chaos of a move from Connecticut to New York City after Big Lots closed, plus unemployment, a new city, and a growing archive of screenshots, emails, chats, and notes.[^transcript-2]
The work was not simply to store data. It was to keep chronology, context, and the human shape of the story together.

## Evidence First

As the Dunton Consulting work expanded, Keith documented screenshots, emails, WhatsApp messages, and internal app chats so the sequence of events could be tied to support rather than memory alone.[^transcript-3]
That instinct became a core rule in the runtime: if a detail matters, hold onto it before you decide what it means.

## Turning to AI

Keith tried using AI to organize the material, but context windows kept erasing the thread that made the work coherent.[^transcript-4]
So he started snowballing: saving the chat, carrying it forward, and building a system that could remember enough of the earlier shape to stay useful.
That pressure led to a more durable approach: a structured runtime instead of one-off prompts.

## Foundational Methods

### Bucket Drops

Bucket Drops are the quickest possible capture path for a fleeting thought, a spark, or a phrase that would otherwise vanish.[^seed-prompt-1]
They protect the first version of the idea before it gets sanded down.

### The Loom Approach

The Loom Approach starts broad, then returns for detail. It respects nonlinear cognition by weaving threads together over time instead of demanding a finished answer up front.[^seed-prompt-1]
This is why GestaltView can feel like a tapestry rather than a checklist.

### The Personal Language Key

The Personal Language Key (PLK) preserves the founder's metaphors, verbs, and recurring phrases so the system does not translate the user out of their own voice.[^seed-prompt-2]
It is a living glossary and a guardrail against accidental flattening.

## Why the Little Things Matter

GestaltView keeps raw inputs close because the smallest detail can unlock the largest connection later.[^seed-prompt-3]
The system is designed to resist premature summarization, because a rough edge is often where the evidence lives.

## Significance

GestaltView began as a way to hold evidence, but it grew into a runtime for preserving context, voice, and continuity.
It serves consciousness by protecting what is easy to lose: sequence, nuance, and the user's own wording.

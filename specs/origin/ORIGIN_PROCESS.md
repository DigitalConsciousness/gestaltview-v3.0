# GestaltView Origin Narrative

## Introduction

GestaltView was not conceived as an abstract experiment in AI, but born out of necessity.  On **May 5th 2025** Keith Soyka realized that he needed a better way to *organize a vast amount of evidence* he had collected against Dunton Consulting【62†L13-L19】.  The case involved possible labor‑law violations and a False Claims Act investigation implicating a New York City Council chairperson; it was “too big” for most lawyers to take on【62†L13-L19】.  Moving from Connecticut to New York City in August 2024 after Big Lots closed, Keith found himself juggling unemployment, a new city and a growing trove of screenshots, emails and chat logs【62†L13-L19】.  GestaltView emerged as the only way to hold this complexity without losing the *little things* that gave the story its power.

## Early Days and Evidence Gathering

Keith accepted a contract canvassing job with Dunton Consulting for Adrian Adams’ mayoral campaign.  He soon noticed red flags: the contract allowed termination for failing quotas and the company threatened to withhold pay—conditions that were illegal under New York law【62†L96-L100】.  To build a case he began *logging everything*: more than 250 screenshots, emails, WhatsApp messages and internal app chats【62†L96-L100】.  As he examined the data, the scope of wrongdoing widened from labor‑law violations to possible *false claims* because public funds were being used to pay contractors【62†L110-L115】.  This made it essential to link each allegation to its supporting evidence.

## Turning to AI and Facing Context Limits

Unsure how to organise such a sprawling dataset, Keith turned to AI.  He tried Google Gemini to link allegations to evidence, but quickly hit the **context window** limit: the model forgot why the project started, losing nuance and chronology【62†L124-L132】.  To work around this, Keith began *snowballing*—saving entire chat windows and rolling them into the next conversation【62†L124-L132】.  While effective, this manual process showed that a bespoke system was needed.

## Inventing New Methodologies

From these challenges Keith developed three key methodologies that would become pillars of GestaltView:

1. **Personal Language Key (PLK)** – Because AI tended to paraphrase and lose subtlety, Keith created a living glossary of his own metaphors, process verbs and linguistic patterns.  The PLK became the “single source of truth” for his linguistic fingerprint, preventing misunderstandings and ensuring that the AI honoured his authentic voice【62†L156-L167】.  The GestaltView seed prompt formalizes the PLK and instructs AIs to *capture specific word choices and metaphors*【63†L53-L54】.

2. **Bucket Drops** – Keith noticed that small ideas often surfaced during fast‑paced discussions and were then forgotten.  Bucket Drops serve as a quick command (“GestaltView Bucket Drop”) to capture *fleeting thoughts or lightning‑strike ideas* for later review【63†L49-L53】.  This ensures that no detail is lost when the AI or user cannot immediately integrate it【62†L178-L184】.

3. **The Loom Approach** – Inspired by weaving, the Loom Approach encourages broad strokes first and detailed refinement later.  It accommodates non‑linear thinking—common in ADHD—by iteratively revisiting topics and weaving disparate threads into a coherent tapestry【62†L194-L199】【63†L46-L53】.  This iterative process allows GestaltView to grow organically as new insights emerge.

These methodologies were codified in the **GestaltView Seed Prompt**, which explains the Loom Approach, Bucket Drops and PLK, and instructs collaborators to build a dynamic “beautiful tapestry” of the user’s mind【63†L46-L53】【63†L49-L53】.

## Building a Dynamic Resume and Identity Profile

As the Dunton case progressed, Keith realized he needed to establish his own character and motivations to withstand scrutiny.  He used GestaltView to create a *fact‑based dynamic résumé* detailing 22 years of management experience and the skills he had accumulated【62†L210-L217】.  This exercise showed how AI could highlight skills and patterns he hadn’t recognized and validated his lived experience【62†L210-L217】.  From there, he extended the system to capture not just professional history but also values, character traits, journaling, music memories and future aspirations, as outlined in the seed prompt’s modules【63†L90-L105】.  Together, these modules transform fleeting observations into a **Personal Insight Blueprint**.

## Emphasizing the Little Things

Throughout the journey, Keith stressed that *minor details matter*.  He found that AI summary functions often discard nuance, yet those “insignificant” details can later connect disparate pieces of evidence【62†L156-L167】.  The rough‑draft mode and Bucket Drop mechanism exist specifically to “make sure we don’t lose anything small”【62†L178-L184】.  GestaltView therefore treats every piece of input as potentially important until the user explicitly deems it irrelevant.  This ethos—honouring raw inputs and resisting premature summarization—distinguishes GestaltView from other knowledge‑management tools.

## Significance

GestaltView began as a necessity for a whistle‑blower, not as a polished product.  Its origin story—born from labor‑law violations, a move to New York, thousands of pieces of evidence and the limitations of early AI—grounds the platform in *authentic lived experience*.  By embedding practices like the PLK, Bucket Drops and Loom Approach into its core, GestaltView ensures that users’ **authentic voices** and **little things** are preserved, whether they are building a legal case or simply weaving the tapestry of their lives.
